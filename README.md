# Computer-Science-Portfolio
A collection of projects and software development work completed throughout my Computer Science program at SNHU.

## CS 230 – Software Design Reflection

### Project Overview

The Gaming Room was the client for this project, and they wanted to expand their game, Draw It or Lose It, beyond its original Android platform. Their goal was to develop a web-based version that players could access from different devices and operating systems. The application needed to support multiple games, teams, and players while making sure names were not duplicated. The design also needed to be flexible enough to support future updates and additional users without having to develop a separate application for each platform.

### What I Did Well

One area I feel I handled well was evaluating the different operating systems and determining which would be the best fit for The Gaming Room. I took the time to consider the advantages and disadvantages of each option, including cost, reliability, performance, and long-term maintenance. Although I am more familiar with Windows, I felt Linux was the better choice for hosting the application. I think I did a good job supporting that recommendation and explaining how it would benefit the client as the game continued to grow.

### How the Design Document Helped

Working through the software design document gave me a better understanding of how much planning goes into developing an application. Before this project, I was more focused on writing code and making sure everything worked correctly. This assignment helped me see why it is important to understand how the different components will interact before development begins. Creating and reviewing the UML diagram was particularly useful because it helped me visualize the relationships between the classes and understand how the application was organized. Having that structure laid out made it easier to see how the code could be maintained and expanded.

### What I Would Improve

If I had the opportunity to revisit one part of this project, I would focus on expanding the system architecture portion of the design. While I addressed the main requirements, I think the document could benefit from a more detailed explanation of how the server would manage multiple players and game sessions at the same time. I would also look further into how the application could handle connection issues or unexpected increases in player activity. Adding those details would provide a clearer picture of how the application could perform under different conditions and help prepare for challenges that might come up later.

### Understanding the User's Needs

Thoughout the project, I tried to approach the design from the client's perspective and consider what they were hoping to accomplish. The Gaming Room wanted to make its game available to a wider audience, so accessibility across different platforms was one of the biggest priorities. I also considered the importance of keeping games, teams, and player information organized so the application would function properly. I believe understanding the user's needs is one of the most important parts of software design. A program might work exactly as it was developed, but that does not necessarily mean it meets the expectations of the people using it. Taking the time to understand those expectations helps guide the design in the right direction.

### My Approach to Software Design

For this project, I approached the design by first identifying the client's requirements and then breaking the work into smaller sections. I looked at how the classes would interact, compared the available operating systems, and considered what resources would be needed to support the application. Working through each section helped me understand how individual design decisions can affect the overall system. In future projects, I would use a similar approach while spending more time reviewing possible challenges before development begins. I would also make better use of diagrams, early testing, and feedback to identify improvements along the way. One of the biggest things I took away from this course is that software design is not just about making something work. It is also about building something that can be maintained, improved, and adapted as requirements change.
